2023 · AE471, with Prof. A. C. Mandal

Identifying Turbulent/Non-Turbulent Interface in a Boundary Layer Data

k = ½(u′2 + w′2),  ω = ∂u/∂z − ∂w/∂x

PIV fields u(x, z, t) over a flat plate: 800 snapshots on an 18,000-point grid (≈ 1.4 × 107 samples), moved from Tecplot into a Python pipeline. From these I built the turbulent kinetic energy, turbulence intensity, total kinetic energy and vorticity fields, then located the interface by thresholding. Regions with total KE above 0.6 of its maximum behave turbulently, and the interface is cleanest for |w′| / U∞ ∈ [0.15, 0.25].

2022 · AE371, with Prof. Sanjay Mittal

Flow Around a Frisbee at AoA 5°

Re = U∞D / ν ≈ 2.84 × 105 (U∞ = 15 m/s)

CFD of an unpowered, axisymmetric, spin-stabilised disc (D = 27.4 cm) at α = 5°. I built the geometry to Potts’ dimensions and an unstructured mesh of 6.9 × 106 nodes and 2.63 × 107 elements, with 20 inflation layers (first cell 0.09 mm; mean skewness 0.186, orthogonal quality 0.814). The case was set up for Prof. Mittal’s in-house FEM solver on the IITK HPC cluster, to be benchmarked against wind-tunnel data.

2024 · ECO613, with Saral Verma

Impact of Covid-19 Pandemic on the Crude Oil and Stock Markets in the US: A Time-Varying Analysis

yt = ct + B1tyt−1 + et,  et ∼ 𝒩(0, At−1ΣtΣtAt−1⊤)

Re-implementation of Liu, Wang and Lee (2020): a TVP-VAR with stochastic volatility (Nakajima, 2011) on yt = (ln cases, Δ ln S&P 500, Δ ln WTI), 106 daily observations from 21 Jan to 6 May 2020. Change-points were located with PELT and stationarity checked by ADF, and the lag s = 1 was chosen by AIC, BIC and HQIC. The parameters βt, at, ht follow random walks, and the posterior is sampled by MCMC (Gibbs with Metropolis–Hastings steps, 104 draws, 103 burn-in) with Geweke diagnostics. The impulse responses show oil and equity returns moving inversely, and both responding slightly but significantly positively to the case shock.

2023 · ECO412, with Saral Verma

General Equilibrium Impact of India’s Accession into CPTPP Using Structural Gravity Model

Xij = (YiEj / Y) · (tij / ΠiPj)1−σ

Anderson–van Wincoop structural gravity on 2021 bilateral flows for N = 20 economies (12 CPTPP members, India, 7 large non-members). The empirical model with exporter and importer fixed effects was estimated by PPML. The counterfactual sets PTAIndia, j = 1 for every member j and re-solves the full-endowment equilibrium in Πi, Pj, pi with a modified version of Herman’s gegravity (NLS) solver. Members get Δ GDP > 0; non-members lose slightly to trade diversion.

2023 · IME625, with S. Indoliya and U. Verma

On Linear Kalman Filter and Its Applications in Finance

Kn = Pn,n−1H⊤(H Pn,n−1H⊤ + Rn)−1

Starts from the scalar gain kn = pn,n−1 / (pn,n−1 + rn), obtained by minimising the posterior variance, and builds up to the five matrix equations. The continuous LTI system ẋ = Ax + Bu is discretised to F = eAΔt. The filter is then applied to a price with state (x, ẋ), F = [1 T; 0 1] and H = [1 0].

2024 · ECO311, group of five

Cycling to School: Increasing Secondary School Enrollment for Girls in India

yihv = β0 + β1F·T·BH + … + εihv

Replication of Muralidharan and Prakash on DLHS-3 household data. The DiD (girls vs boys × cohort 14–15 vs 16–17) rejects parallel trends, so identification uses the triple difference with Jharkhand as a control state. A quadruple difference then adds LDv = 𝟙{distance to school ≥ 3 km}. Result: β̂1 ≈ 0.052 (≈ 32% in relative terms), rising to about 0.0875 where LDv = 1. That points to a lower distance cost as the mechanism.

2024 · ECO413, with P. Dayal and R. Agrawal

On Parameters Influencing Voters’ Preference Dynamics in the Indian Context

accDT = 0.81,  accLR = accRF = 0.74

Two cross-sectional surveys (n = 134 each) of IIT Kanpur Gen Z students, designed along survey-sampling lines to keep the cohort homogeneous. The target is political orientation on five classes, from far-left to far-right, predicted with logistic regression, decision tree, random forest and SVM. With n this small the estimates have high variance, so the ranking is indicative at best.

2022 · AE461, group of four

Aircraft Design: Final Report

WTO = WE + Wf + Wpl = 2552 + 901 + 735 ≈ 4158 kg

Conceptual design of a turboprop utility aircraft. Mission-segment weight fractions give the take-off weight. A matching plot (max speed, take-off run, ceiling, rate of climb) with CD0 = 0.018 sizes the wing loading and the power, which lands in the ~600 shp turboprop class. Then an iterative NACA 4-digit airfoil selection, fuselage and tail sizing, and manoeuvre and gust V–n envelopes.
